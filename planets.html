Why do planets go vakra?

Every planet moves steadily forward around the Sun. Seen from the Earth, some of them seem to stop and walk backward for weeks. Press the button and move your viewpoint from the Sun to the Earth to see why.

Why the loops happen

An inner planet (Budha, Shukra) runs faster than the Earth. When it passes between us and the Sun, it overtakes us and looks to be moving backward. An outer planet (Kuja, Guru, Shani) runs slower. When the Earth overtakes it, it falls behind against the stars, like a slower car seen from a faster one. The time between two vakras is the time for one of them to lap the other: 1 ÷ (1/Tfaster − 1/Tslower).
